On 5 January 2026, one day after my mother's 63rd birthday, she passed away.
She went in to hospital mid-December 2025, and the doctors discovered pancreatic cancer at a stage too late to save her, and she fought (sometimes aware, sometimes not) until the very end.

Rewind to the beginning of 2020. The year of COVID-19, before the pandemic.
I had the opportunity of a life time.
I was able to move to Mauritius for work.
I was excited, and my mom was sad.
We would have video calls daily, keeping in touch and enriching our mornings with each other's company.
We'd do this for the next 6 years.

We spoke about anything and everything.
One day, we spoke about "what if".
"Mom, what do we do if one of us passes away?"
She didn't push the thought away as taboo.
Instead we spoke practically.
It's inevitable and we should cope.
And not be too sad.

So on go the years, with the ebbs and flows of life, work, and play.
And we speak. Relentlessly.

I can't say that I was prepared for death, but I was equipped with the knowledge and mindset to cope with it.
I flew back to South Africa, supported my family, and was by my mom's side until the very end.
I feel warmth and gratitude for being able to do so.

Let's rewind again.
November 2025.
I was burnt out - and badly, from built up stress at work.
I had just moved house for the umpteenth time in the last 6 years.
I had just started a new job, without taking a break.
I had had my appendix removed earlier that year.
It was the anniversary of the passing of my doggy.
My previous landlord ended up in hospital after a serious accident and then sadly passed away.
I was strained by the burdens that were piling up.

When I got back to Mauritius in January, I saw my doctor.
I had a list of 25 serious events that had happened in the last 12 months.
My doctor cried.

Yet, I was doing okay.
Now, read this carefully. I'm not a psychopath. I promise!
I was just coping.
And it was working.

Oh, it most definitely wasn't easy.
My emotional and mental state was remarkably strong, but my body was crashing.
My cortisol levels were super high and my vitamin levels had dropped.
My body was tired and I struggled to concentrate.
And I was sick. Oh boy, was I sick.
And my hands were shaking.
I couldn't get the right amount of sleep.
It was a messy business.

I started doing the right things.
I saw my doctor.
I took the prescribed vitamins.
I ate better.
I gradually started getting better sleep.
I took time off during the day to relax.
And most importantly, **I have been kind to myself**.
And it's paying off!

> "A good life is one in which things do not go perfectly, but you find a way to have a good day regardless." I read this quote from a newsletter by James Clear. It resonates deeply with me.

I'm so grateful for the air I get to breathing.
The water I can drink.
The food I can taste.
The family and friends in my life and the new people I get to meet.
For the sun, shining and the rain falling.

> I could be having a pity party, but instead I choose serendipity.

So what's the secret sauce?
It's nothing revolutionary.
It's just what I leaned on when everything else fell apart.

## Be kind to yourself and others

I've shown up for my family and supported them to the best of my ability.
I've also added a bit of sunshine in the darkest of times through gentle humour and comforting presence.
I had this strength because I was allowing myself to be okay in these trying times.
I didn't feel like a victim, I didn't feel like I was being punished.
I was just practical as life was - well life was just happening.
_C'est la vie_.

## Protect yourself

I could have gone into a spiral.
I could have collapsed from pain, stress and grief.
But of what use would I have been to my family if I were in that state?
I found strength in the little things surrounding me that inherently protected me.

## Don't worry

I've read Dale Carnegie's book "How to Stop Worrying and Start Living".
Worry solves nothing.
It makes us sick.
Stop it.

## Be grateful

Life owes us nothing.
We will have moments of joy and moments of suffering.
How we perceive these moments is up to us.
Reframing the hard times gives us the opportunity to rise, rather than fall.
It doesn't have to be a big thing.
Little gratitudes compound.

## Be mindful

Soak up every moment you have in the present because one day, it will be gone.
Breathe each breath with conviction.
Feel the sun on your skin.
Feel the rain on your face.
Taste your food.
Apologize, forgive, and let go.
Life. Your. Life!

## Plan your next move

Unstuck yourself to avoid paralysis.
Figure out where you need to focus your energy.
Attend to the things within your control.
Write things down.
Put a step-by-step plan in place.
Move forward.

## Find stability in the chaos

There are things around you that remain constant when everything is moving around chaotically.
Hold on to those things.
Perhaps it's a book, a movie, series, song, a person, a place, a routine, a hobby.
Find your stability and hold on to it to get through the storm.

I'm not going to say I had it easy.
I've cried, gotten sick, and often lost my way.
But—I kept going; taking it one moment at a time. With strength, resilience, and grit.
